Controls

Steering: the ship has a real rudder and a turn that builds and decays. Ordering a course (Set, ◀ ▶ buttons or arrow keys) makes the helmsman put rudder on, ease it, then apply counter rudder to stop the swing on the course. Or give rudder orders yourself (P35…S35, , . for 5° port/starboard, M midships): the rate of turn builds slowly and the ship keeps swinging after you go midships, so you must meet the swing with counter rudder, or press Steady as she goes. The bar shows rudder angle: red to the left is port, green to the right is starboard, and the yellow marker is the ordered angle while the rudder is still moving. Rudder is less effective at low speed.

Bridge scales: the top strip is true bearing. Below it, the relative bearing scale counts 0–180° either side of your bow: red = port, green = starboard. Target tags show their relative bearing (P = port, S = starboard).

Radar: the bold white line and arrow show your heading. The blue wedge shows where the bridge view is looking, and its width is the current zoom. The inner ring of red/green numbers is relative bearing.

ECPINS: a chart-style display of AIS targets (open sea has no chart data; the Channel and Malacca show approximate coastlines, banks, ports and the TSS). Drag the chart to pan, use the wheel to zoom, Re-centre to put your ship in the middle again, and Snap to ship to make the chart move with the ship while keeping it wherever you have placed it on screen (turn it off to hold the chart still). Small hollow triangles are sleeping targets; selecting one activates it, showing its vector, past track and the predicted closest point of approach. Press Interrogate (I) to request its AIS static and voyage data (name, MMSI, IMO, call sign, ship type, navigational status, size, draught, destination, ETA) alongside its CPA, TCPA, BCR/BCT and bearing at CPA. Flashing red targets break the CPA/TCPA limits. When a target is predicted to pass inside the ECPINS CPA limit (within the TCPA limit) the Warnings panel (3) opens and an audible alarm beeps until you click Acknowledge for that vessel; each vessel must be acknowledged separately. The Alarms button in the Sim panel (S) turns the alarms and the Warnings menu on or off. Pausing or turning Sound off silences the beeping, but the warning stays until acknowledged.

Locations: choose a Location in the Simulation panel (S). Open sea is the free-roaming sandbox. English Channel is the Dover Strait TSS on an approximate real coastline (SW-bound lane on the English side, NE-bound on the French side, ferries crossing, Goodwin Sands, Dover, Calais…). Strait of Malacca is the strait off Port Dickson / Cape Rachado and Rupat Island, with a dense TSS and deep-draught tankers, tugs with tows, anchored ships, unlit fishing boats and dhows that may not follow the Rules (no or dim lights, no AIS, weak radar echo, erratic courses, failing to give way). Land shows on the bridge horizon, the radar and ECPINS (running aground stops the engine). With rule assessment on, your own Rule 10 status appears under Own ship. Coastlines are approximate, not for navigation.

Vertical scale on the bridge view is exaggerated so lights and shapes are readable. Lights are limited by their COLREGS range and the visibility setting; the radar always sees out to its range.
